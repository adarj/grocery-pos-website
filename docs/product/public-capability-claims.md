# Public capability claims

Grocery POS is positioned around Store Sovereignty, Grocery Depth,
Global-by-Design, Verified Reliability, Certified Interoperability,
Progressive Power, and Managed Freedom.

Representative direction is “Grocery depth without enterprise baggage,” “Cloud
convenience without cloud dependence,” “Managed hardware without hardware
captivity,” and “Professional support without opaque lock-in.” These strategic
pillars and positioning are not evidence that a particular feature, certification,
service, or guarantee is commercially available.

## Maturity policy

A later capability ledger will distinguish readiness/availability, separately from
publication authorization:

| Status | Meaning and treatment if publication is authorized |
| --- | --- |
| AVAILABLE | Released and supported for a defined audience/scope; state material market, hardware, or service limitations |
| PILOT | In bounded real-customer use; disclose participation/access and scope limits rather than implying general availability |
| PREVIEW | Offered for evaluation before general release; disclose maturity, access, and support limitations |
| PLANNED | Roadmap/design intent; describe as future intent, without asserting current functionality or inventing delivery dates |
| INTERNAL | Internal-only capability/work; do not advertise it as a customer offering |

This checkpoint defines statuses and publication policy only. It creates no
exhaustive capability database and assigns no status to unverified features.

## Publication authorization

Maturity describes product readiness or availability; status alone never grants
permission to disclose information publicly. Publication requires a separate
authoritative, owner-maintained decision approving the information for its intended
public audience. INTERNAL does not mean safe to describe merely by labeling it internal.

Confidential, security-sensitive, contractual, partner-restricted, embargoed, or
otherwise non-public capability information remains non-public regardless of
maturity wording. If publication/disclosure approval is absent, unclear, or
conflicting, do not publish the information.

## Evidence before publication

Future Codex-generated marketing/content must be checked against authoritative,
owner-maintained product-status information and separate publication/disclosure
approval. Record the sources and applicable audience/market/version when reviewing
a claim. Architecture documents, aspirational positioning, and an implementation
branch alone establish neither availability nor permission to publish.

If evidence is absent or conflicting, mark the claim unresolved for review and do
not publish it as AVAILABLE; do not guess a roadmap status either. Keep pilot,
preview, and planned qualifiers next to the relevant claim. Reliability,
interoperability, certification, offline behavior, and support promises require
evidence for the actual advertised scope. Review existing copy when status changes.
